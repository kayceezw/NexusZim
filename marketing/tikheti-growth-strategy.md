## PART 2: EVENTBRITE PRICING MODEL

### Current Live Pricing (scraped from eventbrite.com/pricing, July 2026)

**Free events:** No fees. Unlimited free events at no cost.

**Paid events:**
- Service fee: **3.7% + $1.79 per ticket**
- Payment processing fee: **2.9% per order**
- Total effective take rate: **~6.6%+ per ticket** (higher on lower-priced tickets due to the flat $1.79)

**On a $10 ticket:**
- Service fee: $0.37 + $1.79 = $2.16
- Payment processing: $0.29
- Total fees: ~$2.45 (24.5% of ticket price)
- This is why low-price events are particularly expensive on Eventbrite

**On a $50 ticket:**
- Service fee: $1.85 + $1.79 = $3.64
- Payment processing: $1.45
- Total fees: ~$5.09 (10.2% of ticket price)

**Email marketing add-on (Eventbrite Pro):** $15/month for up to 10,000 daily emails

**Enterprise/large events:** Custom pricing through sales

**Critical note for Tikheti:** Eventbrite's pricing page explicitly states availability only in **"the United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, the United Kingdom and Ireland."** Africa is not a supported market. They have event listings in Harare but no official organiser payment infrastructure there. This is Tikheti's biggest structural advantage.

### How Fees Are Handled

By default, fees are charged to ticket buyers (added on top of the ticket price). Organisers can optionally absorb fees. This means the organiser quotes one price but the buyer pays more — a common source of consumer complaints about "hidden fees at checkout."

## PART 3: EVENTBRITE'S GROWTH STORY — THE ACTUAL TACTICS

*Source: First Round Capital interview with Kevin and Julia Hartz*

### The Long Tail Insight

Eventbrite's founding insight was identifying the **"long tail of events"** — small events (cooking classes, yoga seminars, community meetings, food festivals) that Ticketmaster would never touch. They did not try to compete with Ticketmaster for stadium concerts. They went where incumbents wouldn't.

**Tikheti parallel:** The Harare market's long tail is enormous — church fundraisers, school events, community gatherings, small concerts, comedy nights, business workshops. This is exactly where Tikheti should start before going after stadium events.

### The Freemium Flywheel

Eventbrite's model: **free for free events, fee-based for paid events.** This was the core growth engine.

- Free events created enormous volume of indexed pages
- Those pages drove organic Google traffic
- Attendees of free events discovered the platform
- A measurable percentage of attendees converted to paid organisers
- Each new organiser brought more attendees
- Each new attendee was a potential future organiser

They bootstrapped for 2 years on under $250,000 before raising their first $6.5M. The freemium model reduced their customer acquisition cost toward zero — growth was organic and compounding.

### The SEO Growth Hack

This is Eventbrite's single most powerful growth lever. Every event page created on Eventbrite was indexed by Google. When someone searched "yoga class Harare" or "comedy night Bulawayo," Eventbrite pages appeared in results.

Specific data from the Google Search case study:
- After implementing structured data markup on all event pages, Eventbrite saw a **100% increase in typical year-over-year growth** of traffic from Google Search to their event listing pages
- They now have over **10 million indexed pages** — requiring multiple sitemaps (Google's limit is 50,000 URLs per sitemap)
- Eventbrite is one of Google's top 500 most trusted sites — the **only ticketing platform** at that domain authority level
- They use internal linking on popular pages to direct Google crawlers to pages they want indexed
- Quality control: event pages are initially gated from Google indexing and only released after passing their trust and safety algorithm

**Tikheti parallel:** Every Tikheti event page must be SEO-optimised from day one. "Concert Harare July 2026", "comedy show Bulawayo tickets", "wedding venue Harare" — these search terms should return Tikheti event pages. This compounds over time into the most durable growth asset Tikheti will build.

### The Attendee-to-Organiser Flywheel

Eventbrite discovered that a significant number of their event organisers **first found the platform as a ticket buyer.** They identified this loop, ran a six-month test with a small team (three engineers, one part-time PM, one growth lead), and confirmed it was driving organic growth.

The mechanism:
1. Organiser promotes event → attendees discover Eventbrite
2. Attendee buys ticket → experiences the platform as a buyer
3. Attendee later needs to organise an event → remembers Eventbrite
4. They become an organiser → promote to their own network
5. Cycle repeats

Eventbrite-driven ticket sales now represent **30% of total tickets sold** — meaning nearly a third of all sales come from people who discovered the event through Eventbrite's own discovery features, not the organiser's marketing.

**Tikheti parallel:** Every event-goer who has a good ticket-buying experience is a future Tikheti organiser. This means the buyer experience — seamless EcoCash payment, clean digital ticket, professional email confirmation — is not a nice-to-have. It is the organiser acquisition funnel.

### The 2008–2009 Recession Pivot

When the economy collapsed in 2008–2009, Eventbrite found an unexpected benefit: **unemployed professionals used the platform to teach skills and monetise expertise.** Cooking classes, skill-sharing workshops, DIY education events flourished. The economic downturn that killed many startups fed Eventbrite's growth.

**Tikheti parallel:** Zimbabwe's economic environment — currency volatility, informal employment, skills-based entrepreneurship — creates the same dynamic. People with skills are monetising them through events. Tikheti should actively court this segment: professional development workshops, skills training events, income-generating community gatherings.

## PART 5: EVENTBRITE'S WEAKNESSES — WHERE TIKHETI WINS

These are pulled from organiser reviews, Reddit threads, and platform comparison sites (2024–2025 data):

### Weakness 1: Fees Kill Low-Price Events
Effective fees of 24%+ on a $10 ticket make Eventbrite uneconomical for community events, church fundraisers, and affordable cultural events. Organisers of lower-price events are the most price-sensitive and most likely to switch.

**Tikheti opportunity:** A lower flat fee or percentage that makes small community events viable. Even matching Quicket's 4.9% total would be dramatically better than Eventbrite for Zimbabwe's price point.

### Weakness 2: Holds Money Until After the Event
Eventbrite holds ticket sale funds until after the event is completed. For an organiser who needs to pay a venue deposit, caterer, and sound technician before the event, this is catastrophic. This is the **number one complaint** in organiser reviews: "the company is holding their money as if it were theirs."

**Tikheti opportunity: Pre-event payouts.** Pay organisers before the event. This is a structural competitive advantage that NexusZim2's Quicket-style model confirms (Quicket also offered early payouts as a differentiator). In Zimbabwe, where organisers often need cash upfront for vendors who do not extend credit, this is not a nice-to-have — it is a market requirement.

### Weakness 3: AI-Only Customer Support
Eventbrite's support has degraded to an AI chatbot that users describe as "useless." When an organiser has a problem (account locked, fraud, payout issue, VAT query), there is no human to call.

**Tikheti opportunity:** A real WhatsApp support line. A human answers within business hours. This is operationally inexpensive at early scale and creates a profound brand differentiation: "Tikheti actually picks up."

### Weakness 4: Fraud and Fake Events
Eventbrite's open platform allows fraudulent event listings. Event-goers have bought tickets to events that did not exist or were misrepresented. This undermines trust for legitimate organisers on the platform.

**Tikheti opportunity:** A verified events layer. Not necessarily as rigorous as NexusZim2's provider verification, but enough to screen: organiser identity, venue confirmation, deposit receipt. A "Tikheti Verified Event" badge that tells buyers this event has been lightly screened.

### Weakness 5: Heavy Eventbrite Branding on Organiser Pages
Eventbrite's branding appears prominently on all event pages and in all emails sent to attendees. After buying a ticket, Eventbrite promotes other events to the attendee — effectively using the organiser's audience to market the platform and other events.

**Tikheti opportunity:** White-label or lightly branded event pages where the organiser's brand is primary. Do not market other events to an organiser's attendees without permission.

### Weakness 6: Not Built for Africa
Eventbrite does not officially support African payment methods. No EcoCash, no mobile money integration, no ZWG/USD dual-currency awareness, no understanding of the Zimbabwean context. Their Harare listing page shows 4 events — borderline irrelevant.

**This is the most important weakness.** It is structural, not operational. Eventbrite cannot fix this quickly because their infrastructure is built for Western payment rails. Tikheti is built for Africa from the start.

No platform operating in Zimbabwe currently advertises pre-event payout as a feature. All local platforms accept payments but none appears to have built EcoCash as a first-class, frictionless checkout experience from the ground up. Tikheti, built on Supabase with a mobile-first stack, can implement EcoCash as the primary flow — not an afterthought.

No current Zimbabwe platform issues tickets via WhatsApp natively. WhatsApp is where Zimbabweans live. A ticket confirmation + QR code arriving directly in WhatsApp rather than an email the buyer may not see is a significant UX advantage.

Webtickets' South African infrastructure, local platforms' unclear international payment support, and Eventbrite's non-presence create a clear lane for Tikheti to serve diaspora bookings as a first-class feature.

**What Eventbrite did:** Made free events completely free. This flooded the platform with event pages, drove SEO, and converted attendees to organisers.

**What Eventbrite did:** User-generated event pages indexed by Google drove massive organic growth. Adding structured data markup doubled their year-over-year traffic growth from Google.

**How Tikheti applies it:** Every single event page on Tikheti must be:

**What Eventbrite did wrong:** Replaced human support with AI chatbots. This is now their #1 complaint and a reason organisers switch.

"I had a problem with my event and Tikheti sorted it out on WhatsApp within an hour" is a testimonial that travels far in tight Harare business networks.

### Lesson 10: The Verified Event Badge — Tikheti's Trust Layer

**What Eventbrite failed to do:** Let fake events proliferate on the platform, eroding trust for legitimate organisers and damaging the buyer experience.

**What Tikheti builds:** A lightweight "Tikheti Verified Event" badge for events that have passed a basic screening (organiser identity confirmed, venue booked, deposit paid, event has previously run successfully on the platform). Not as rigorous as NexusZim2's provider verification — but enough to distinguish:
- ✓ Verified events: light screening, trust badge, featured placement
- Standard events: self-submitted, no badge

This creates a quality signal, incentivises serious organisers to complete verification, and gives buyers a meaningful trust layer. In a market with event deposit scams, this is a functional safety feature, not marketing.
